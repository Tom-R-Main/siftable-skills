# Evaluation rubric

Score the finished prose after the final revision. A passing draft needs at least 85 points and no critical failure.

## Critical failures

Any one of these requires revision regardless of score:

- adds or invents a fact, number, date, name, quote, citation, source, event, result, or personal experience;
- changes the meaning, causal strength, certainty, scope, or attribution of a material claim;
- removes a qualification that affects interpretation;
- alters quoted material, citations, links, code, commands, identifiers, or required terminology;
- presents an opinion as fact or assigns an unstated opinion to the author;
- imitates the voice so aggressively that the result becomes parody;
- damages the genre, such as making legal prose chatty or technical instructions ambiguous;
- follows a surface heuristic despite contrary evidence in the author's sample.

## 1. Factual and semantic fidelity: 25 points

**25:** Every material claim survives with the same scope and certainty. Immutable material is exact. No new information appears.

**20:** Meaning is intact, but one minor qualification or attribution could be clearer.

**15:** The central claim survives, but compression or restructuring weakens a secondary point.

**10 or less:** A claim changed, evidence was overstated, or the rewrite introduced unsupported detail.

## 2. Voice fidelity: 20 points

**20:** The prose matches the author's register, stance, sentence behavior, punctuation, diction, and reader relationship without caricature.

**16:** The voice is recognizably aligned but smooths out one or two recurring habits.

**12:** The tone is appropriate to the genre but generic rather than author-specific.

**8 or less:** The text sounds like a different writer, a marketing template, or an exaggerated imitation.

## 3. Purpose, audience, and genre fit: 15 points

**15:** The piece performs the requested job, assumes the right reader knowledge, and follows the channel's conventions.

**12:** The purpose is clear, but the level of explanation or tone is slightly mismatched.

**9:** The prose is readable but could be sent to almost any audience.

**6 or less:** It misses the requested action, format, or genre.

## 4. Specificity and explanatory value: 15 points

**15:** Concrete, source-grounded details carry the argument. Abstractions are defined or necessary. Causal claims include a mechanism when the source supports one.

**12:** Mostly specific, with a few generic sentences or unused details.

**9:** Several claims announce importance or improvement without explaining how.

**6 or less:** The prose relies on vague authority, filler, or invented specificity.

## 5. Rhythm and sentence craft: 10 points

**10:** Sentence length and structure vary with the thought. Emphasis feels earned. The passage reads naturally aloud.

**8:** Minor repetition in openings, syntax, or paragraph endings.

**6:** Noticeably metronomic, choppy, or overextended in places.

**4 or less:** Mechanical cadence, stacked fragments, or uniformly dense sentences interfere with reading.

## 6. Structural naturalness: 10 points

**10:** Paragraphs have distinct functions and appropriate lengths. Transitions arise from the argument. The opening starts at the right point and the ending stops at the right point.

**8:** Structure is sound but slightly over-signposted or symmetrical.

**6:** The piece follows a visible template, repeats headings, or includes a generic conclusion.

**4 or less:** Organization obscures the point or preserves a weak source structure without reason.

## 7. Surface restraint: 5 points

**5:** Formatting, punctuation, emphasis, lists, and watched vocabulary fit the author and destination.

**4:** One or two cosmetic habits repeat but do not distract.

**3:** Decorative bold, title case, dashes, transitions, or stock vocabulary become noticeable.

**2 or less:** Surface patterning dominates the prose.

## Revision rule

When the score falls below 85, revise the lowest two categories first. Run the critical-failure check again after every revision. Do not improve style by changing the facts.
