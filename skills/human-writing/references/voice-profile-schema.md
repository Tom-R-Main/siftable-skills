# Voice profile schema

Use this schema when the user asks for a reusable profile of an author's writing. Base every conclusion on the supplied samples. Separate stable tendencies from context-specific behavior.

## Profile metadata

- **Author or profile label:**
- **Sample types:**
- **Sample dates, when known:**
- **Genres and audiences represented:**
- **Coverage limits:**
- **Overall confidence:** high, medium, or low, with one sentence explaining why.

## Communication posture

Describe the author's usual relationship with the reader. Note whether the writer sounds peer-to-peer, instructional, adversarial, intimate, executive, journalistic, academic, or mixed. Record how directly the writer states disagreement, requests, uncertainty, and emotion.

## Register and diction

Record:

- plain versus specialized vocabulary;
- contractions, slang, profanity, idioms, and era-bound references;
- preferred technical terms and words the author repeats intentionally;
- typical intensity and tolerance for promotional language;
- phrases that recur often enough to be meaningful.

Do not treat a single unusual word as a stable habit.

## Sentence behavior

Record:

- typical sentence length and observed range;
- use of fragments, run-ons, coordination, subordination, and parenthetical clauses;
- common openings and transitions;
- cadence under explanation, persuasion, humor, and disagreement;
- whether short sentences are routine or reserved for emphasis.

## Paragraph and document behavior

Record:

- paragraph length and density;
- use of headings, lists, examples, and topic sentences;
- how the author opens, changes direction, qualifies a claim, and ends;
- whether the organization is linear, associative, narrative, or modular.

## Punctuation and typography

Record observed use of commas, semicolons, colons, parentheses, dashes, quotation marks, ellipses, exclamation points, capitalization, bold, and italics. Distinguish author choices from the editor or platform when possible.

## Reasoning and evidence

Record whether the author tends to:

- lead with a claim or build toward it;
- use figures, sources, anecdotes, examples, analogies, or counterexamples;
- state confidence explicitly;
- preserve mixed conclusions or resolve them;
- name causal mechanisms or rely on compressed assertions.

## Personality signals

Describe supported humor, warmth, impatience, skepticism, enthusiasm, self-correction, asides, and unresolved tension. Do not invent personality traits from neutral professional prose.

## Preserve

List five to ten behaviors that should survive editing. Each item should cite a short sample fragment or describe a repeated pattern.

## Avoid

List patterns that would make the output unlike the author. Include generic model habits only when they conflict with the samples.

## Situational or uncertain

List behaviors that appear in only one genre, one period, or one sample. Do not promote them to global rules without more evidence.

## Operational prompt

End with a compact instruction block that another model can use. It should describe tendencies, exceptions, and confidence without copying long passages from the samples or turning the profile into a caricature.
