# Pattern catalog

This catalog supports the `human-writing` skill. It is diagnostic, not a blacklist. A pattern deserves revision when it obscures meaning, repeats across the piece, conflicts with the author's voice, or appears as part of a larger cluster.

## How to use the catalog

For each suspected pattern:

1. Identify the job the sentence is trying to do.
2. Decide whether the pattern helps that job in this genre and voice.
3. Preserve the claim, evidence, and qualification.
4. Repair the structure rather than swapping one watched word for a synonym.
5. Read the neighboring sentences. Many problems arise from repetition across a passage, not from a single sentence.

## 1. Inflated significance

**Signals**

- stands as, serves as, testament, pivotal, crucial, enduring legacy;
- marks a turning point, reflects a broader shift, underscores the importance;
- claims that an ordinary feature reshapes a field without evidence.

**Why it fails**

The sentence tells the reader how important something is instead of naming the consequence that makes it important.

**Repair**

State the event, scale, mechanism, result, or decision. Cut the significance claim when the source does not support it.

## 2. Promotional gloss

**Signals**

- vibrant, breathtaking, renowned, seamless, intuitive, powerful, robust;
- boasts, unlocks, empowers, elevates, transforms;
- broad praise with no comparative or observable basis.

**Why it fails**

The prose sounds like unsourced marketing. It also displaces useful detail.

**Repair**

Name what the product, place, or process does. Keep evaluative language only when it belongs to the author's stated opinion or a cited source.

## 3. Fake analytical tails

**Signals**

- comma plus "highlighting," "underscoring," "reflecting," "ensuring," "fostering," or "contributing";
- an interpretive suffix that restates the sentence at a higher level of abstraction.

**Why it fails**

The sentence performs analysis without supplying a mechanism or new claim.

**Repair**

Turn the interpretation into a separate proposition and explain the link. If no link can be stated from the source, remove the tail.

## 4. Copula avoidance

**Signals**

- serves as, stands as, functions as, represents a, boasts, features;
- elaborate verbs used to avoid "is," "are," "has," or "contains."

**Why it fails**

Simple relationships become ceremonial or vague.

**Repair**

Use the plain copula or possession verb when it states the relationship more accurately. Keep the fuller verb when it adds meaning.

## 5. Vague attribution

**Signals**

- experts say, observers note, critics argue, industry reports suggest;
- "research shows" without an identified study when the source should be available.

**Why it fails**

The sentence borrows authority while hiding the source.

**Repair**

Name the person, group, study, or document. Narrow the statement to what the available source supports. Remove it when no source exists.

## 6. Passive voice that hides responsibility

**Signals**

- a decision was made, mistakes were made, concerns were raised;
- an important action appears without an actor even though the source names one.

**Why it fails**

The reader cannot tell who acted or who bears responsibility.

**Repair**

Put the relevant actor in the subject position. Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, immaterial, already obvious, deliberately protected, or conventionally omitted.

## 7. Elliptical or subjectless fragments

**Signals**

- "No configuration needed," "Results preserved automatically," or similar fragments used as ordinary explanatory prose;
- a sequence of labels masquerading as sentences.

**Why it fails**

The missing subject can make instructions vague or give the passage a clipped product-demo cadence.

**Repair**

Name the actor when it improves clarity: "You do not need a configuration file" or "The system preserves the results." Keep fragments in interfaces, headings, notes, dialogue, and voices that use them naturally.

## 8. False agency

**Signals**

- data tells, markets reward, conversations move, decisions emerge, culture decides;
- an abstraction performs a human choice without naming the people involved.

**Why it fails**

The sentence replaces a mechanism with a metaphor and often conceals responsibility.

**Repair**

Name the reader, team, buyer, regulator, researcher, or other actor when the source permits it. Keep ordinary lexicalized metaphors when they are clear and natural.

## 9. Abstract noun stacks

**Signals**

- implementation of the optimization of the coordination process;
- several nominalizations with few concrete subjects or verbs.

**Why it fails**

The reader must reconstruct who did what.

**Repair**

Restore actors and verbs. Preserve domain terms that carry precise technical or legal meaning.

## 10. False ranges

**Signals**

- "from X to Y" where X and Y are unrelated categories;
- "everything from" followed by a decorative assortment rather than a scale.

**Why it fails**

The construction implies coverage or progression that the examples do not establish.

**Repair**

List the actual topics, or define the real axis of variation.

## 11. Formulaic negative contrast

**Signals**

- not X, but Y;
- it is not just X, it is Y;
- X is not the problem. Y is.

**Why it fails**

Frequent use creates predictable setup-and-reveal prose. Sometimes the negated misconception is not one the reader held.

**Repair**

State Y directly. Keep the contrast when correcting a real misconception or distinguishing two live alternatives.

## 12. Negative runway

**Signals**

- not this, not that, not the other thing, but Z;
- several sentences describing what something is not before naming it.

**Why it fails**

The point arrives late and with manufactured drama.

**Repair**

Lead with Z. Retain only the exclusions that prevent a likely misunderstanding.

## 13. Forced rule of three

**Signals**

- repeated three-item lists across a piece;
- a weak third item added for completeness or cadence.

**Why it fails**

The structure becomes audible and encourages padding.

**Repair**

Use the number of items the content requires. Keep three when there are exactly three categories, steps, findings, or constraints.

## 14. Rhetorical setup

**Signals**

- What if I told you...?
- Here is what I mean.
- Think about it.
- a question answered immediately with an obvious claim.

**Why it fails**

The prose announces insight instead of delivering it.

**Repair**

Start with the claim. Keep a genuine question when the question itself frames the inquiry or remains open.

## 15. Persuasive-authority preface

**Signals**

- the real question is, at its core, fundamentally, what really matters;
- phrases that promise a deeper truth before an ordinary point.

**Why it fails**

The preface spends authority the sentence has not earned.

**Repair**

Remove the preface and sharpen the actual claim.

## 16. Manufactured punchline

**Signals**

- several short declarative fragments in sequence;
- every paragraph ends with a quotable one-liner;
- "That's it. That's the point."

**Why it fails**

Cadence substitutes for substance and quickly becomes mechanical.

**Repair**

Combine related fragments. Reserve short sentences for moments that deserve contrast or emphasis.

## 17. Aphorism template

**Signals**

- X is the language, currency, architecture, or mirror of Y;
- ordinary claims recast as universal maxims.

**Why it fails**

The metaphor sounds portable but often reduces precision.

**Repair**

State the observable relationship. Keep the metaphor when it is original, apt, and consistent with the author's voice.

## 18. Metronomic sentences

**Signals**

- several sentences share nearly identical length and syntax;
- repeated subject-verb-object declarations;
- each sentence resolves with the same cadence.

**Why it fails**

The passage feels generated from a template even when every sentence is grammatical.

**Repair**

Rebuild the passage around relationships among ideas. Combine dependent claims, vary clause structure, and let emphasis determine length.

## 19. Uniform paragraph geometry

**Signals**

- every paragraph has the same number of sentences;
- every section begins with a generic topic sentence and ends with a summary line.

**Why it fails**

The document looks assembled rather than argued or narrated.

**Repair**

Give each paragraph one real function. Let the evidence determine its size.

## 20. Elegant variation

**Signals**

- app, application, platform, solution, and tool used in rotation for the same object;
- protagonist, hero, central figure, and main character cycled mechanically.

**Why it fails**

Terminology becomes unstable and the prose calls attention to word choice.

**Repair**

Repeat the correct noun. Use a synonym only when the meaning or emphasis changes.

## 21. Transition stacking

**Signals**

- additionally, moreover, furthermore, consequently at the start of many paragraphs;
- first, second, finally imposed on material that is not procedural.

**Why it fails**

The transitions expose the outline rather than the relationship between ideas.

**Repair**

Use a transition only when the relationship would otherwise be unclear. Often the content can create the transition itself.

## 22. Signposting and announcements

**Signals**

- let's dive in, let's explore, this section examines, the rest of this essay will;
- a heading followed by a sentence that restates the heading.

**Why it fails**

The document pauses to explain that it is a document.

**Repair**

Begin the explanation. Keep a roadmap in long technical, academic, or legal work when it genuinely helps navigation.

## 23. Chat residue

**Signals**

- Certainly, Of course, Great question, I hope this helps;
- Let me know if you would like more;
- offers, reassurances, and apologies copied into the artifact.

**Why it fails**

The prose retains the interaction that produced it.

**Repair**

Remove the wrapper. Preserve only communication that belongs in the requested channel.

## 24. Sycophancy

**Signals**

- excessive agreement, praise, or validation before analysis;
- "You are absolutely right" when the claim needs qualification.

**Why it fails**

It weakens trust and can distort the substance.

**Repair**

Acknowledge the relevant point in proportion to the evidence, then answer directly.

## 25. Knowledge-gap filler

**Signals**

- while details are limited, it appears, likely, maintains a low profile;
- invented biography or plausible context used to cover missing information.

**Why it fails**

The prose converts absence of evidence into a story.

**Repair**

State what is unknown, identify the missing source, or omit the passage. Never fill the gap with a guess presented as fact.

## 26. Excessive hedging

**Signals**

- could potentially possibly, may perhaps, might tend to;
- qualifiers stacked without a distinct epistemic purpose.

**Why it fails**

The sentence becomes evasive and hard to interpret.

**Repair**

Choose the one modal or qualifier that matches the evidence. Keep multiple qualifications when they represent different uncertainties.

## 27. Empty intensifiers and adverbs

**Signals**

- really, truly, deeply, incredibly, significantly without a measure or reason;
- adverbs that repeat the verb's meaning.

**Why it fails**

The modifier asks the reader to feel force that the sentence has not supplied.

**Repair**

Use a stronger or more precise verb, add the supporting detail, or remove the modifier. Keep adverbs that carry necessary meaning, timing, frequency, scope, or attitude.

## 28. Generic challenge and outlook sections

**Signals**

- "Despite these challenges" followed by predictable optimism;
- a future section containing no plans, dates, decisions, or unresolved questions.

**Why it fails**

The section completes an outline template rather than the argument.

**Repair**

Name the actual constraint and response. Remove the section when the source has no evidence for it.

## 29. Generic conclusions

**Signals**

- the future is bright, exciting times lie ahead, well positioned for growth;
- a closing paragraph that restates the thesis without adding consequence.

**Why it fails**

The piece ends after its substantive ending.

**Repair**

Stop on the last concrete fact, decision, implication, tension, or next action.

## 30. Decorative formatting

**Signals**

- bold applied to every key noun;
- emoji-led headings;
- title case by default;
- repeated inline-header bullets that could be sentences or a table.

**Why it fails**

Formatting becomes a substitute for hierarchy.

**Repair**

Use the native conventions of the destination. Keep lists and emphasis when they improve scanning or encode real structure.

## 31. Diff narration

**Signals**

- documentation describes what was recently added or changed rather than how the current system works;
- comments depend on knowledge of the previous implementation.

**Why it fails**

The prose ages poorly and does not stand alone.

**Repair**

Describe the present behavior. Keep change-oriented language in changelogs, release notes, migration guides, and postmortems.

## 32. Overcorrection

**Signals**

- removing every dash, adverb, passive construction, question, or long sentence;
- adding fragments, typos, slang, or fake uncertainty to look human;
- simplifying precise material into vague casual prose.

**Why it fails**

The resulting prose follows a different template and may damage accuracy or voice.

**Repair**

Treat all surface patterns as contextual. Preserve purposeful choices and revise only what weakens the piece.

## 33. Mechanical compound styling

**Signals**

- every compound receives a hyphen regardless of position;
- predicate phrases such as "the report is high-quality" repeat throughout the piece;
- clusters of corporate compounds make ordinary prose feel templated.

**Why it fails**

Mechanical consistency can conflict with normal usage and the author's habits. The issue is not the existence of compounds but their repetitive, indiscriminate styling.

**Repair**

Follow the destination's style guide. Hyphenate compounds when grammar or clarity calls for it, especially before a noun, and avoid treating every familiar phrase as a fixed compound.
