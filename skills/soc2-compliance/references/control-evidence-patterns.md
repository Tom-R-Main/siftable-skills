# Control and evidence patterns

Use these as design prompts, not as a universal control catalog. Tailor every control to scope, commitments, criteria, risk, architecture, and auditor expectations.

## Evidence quality

Strong evidence is:

- generated by or reconciled to the system of record;
- attributable to a performer or automated identity;
- time-bounded to the relevant date or period;
- complete for the stated population;
- precise enough to show the control result;
- retained and protected from unauthorized alteration;
- reproducible from a documented query, export, or workflow.

For each artifact, record who produced it, how, from which system, for what period, and how completeness and accuracy were checked.

## Common patterns

| Control surface | Control design questions | Typical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governance and risk | Who approves policy, owns risks, and monitors remediation? | Approved policies, risk register, meeting records, exception decisions |
| Workforce access | How are joiner, mover, leaver, privileged, and periodic reviews controlled? | HR-to-IdP population, tickets, role changes, access-review signoff, termination timing |
| Authentication | Where are MFA, SSO, session, credential, and break-glass requirements enforced? | IdP configuration export, policy, exception list, break-glass test |
| Change management | How are changes authorized, tested, reviewed, deployed, and rolled back? | Pull requests, approvals, CI results, deployment records, emergency-change review |
| Vulnerability management | How are assets covered, findings prioritized, exceptions approved, and remediation verified? | Asset/scanner reconciliation, scan exports, tickets, SLA aging, retest results |
| Logging and monitoring | Which events are collected, retained, protected, reviewed, and alerted on? | Source inventory, retention/config exports, rule changes, alerts, review records |
| Incident response | Are roles, severity, escalation, communications, evidence preservation, and lessons learned operational? | Plan, exercise, incident tickets, timeline, communications, post-incident actions |
| Backup and recovery | Do backups cover scoped data, and are restoration and recovery objectives tested? | Job reports, immutable settings, restore tests, exercise results, exceptions |
| Availability | Are capacity, redundancy, monitoring, continuity, and recovery commitments testable? | SLO reports, outage records, capacity reviews, DR tests, corrective actions |
| Data protection | Are classification, encryption, key management, retention, and disposal enforced? | Data inventory, KMS settings, key rotation, retention jobs, deletion records |
| Vendor risk | Are critical vendors inventoried, risk-assessed, contracted, monitored, and offboarded? | Vendor register, due diligence, reports, contracts, reviews, remediation |
| Physical security | How are facilities and media access restricted and monitored? | Badge reports, visitor logs, camera/guard procedures, media disposal |

## Population completeness

An auditor may sample from the full population. Establish the population before selecting evidence:

- define the event that creates a population member;
- export from the authoritative system;
- reconcile totals to a second source when practical;
- document filters, time zones, exclusions, and failed or canceled events;
- preserve the export and query parameters.

For low-frequency controls, show every occurrence. For no-occurrence controls, prove the absence from a reliable population rather than asserting “none.”

## Exceptions

Record the requirement, observed condition, cause, duration, affected population, risk, compensating control, remediation, and retest. Do not erase an exception by rewriting the control after the fact.

## Screenshots

Use screenshots to show UI-only state or corroborate an export. Include the system, tenant, timestamp, scope, and visible setting. Prefer machine-readable exports, audit logs, tickets, or API results when available.
