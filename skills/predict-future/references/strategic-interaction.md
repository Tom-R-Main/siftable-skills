# Strategic Interaction Lens

Use this lens only when an outcome depends materially on actors adapting to one another. It is a mechanism generator, not a universal theory of human behavior and not a probability model.

## Trigger test

Use the lens when most of these are true:

- identifiable actors or coalitions can choose among materially different moves;
- one actor's outcome depends on what others choose;
- actors can observe, anticipate, signal, bargain, deter, coordinate, or retaliate;
- incentives, information, power, or institutional rules constrain the moves;
- adaptation could change the forecast more than a purely technical trend would.

Typical cases include standards contests, platform competition, regulation and lobbying, market entry and incumbent response, adoption coordination, negotiations, delegated authority, principal-agent problems, and repeated cooperation.

Skip the lens when physical constraints, random shocks, demographics, or non-strategic execution dominate. Do not turn every human choice into a game.

## Composition contract

Use the compact pass below for forecast-only questions. When the user also has a consequential strategic decision and `$strategic-decision-mapping` is installed, use the full related skill alongside `predict-future`. Its workflow may produce a recommendation and next turn; place those under decision implications.

From its decision map, extract:

1. actors and coalitions;
2. materially available moves;
3. incentives, outside options, information, power, and institutional constraints;
4. likely countermoves and adaptations;
5. commitments, signals, and their cost to fake;
6. observable indicators and break conditions;
7. sensitivity to changed assumptions.

Return those results to the forecast's mechanism map. `predict-future` alone owns the probability, evidence synthesis, ensemble aggregation, resolution rule, ledger version, and score. A strategic recommendation is not evidence for the forecast that motivated it.

If there is no strategic decision or the related skill is not installed, perform the compact pass below. The forecast remains usable without the dependency.

## Compact pass

Record:

| element | question |
|---|---|
| Actors | Who can materially alter the outcome? Are coalitions more accurate than named organizations? |
| Moves | What can each actor actually do before the resolution date? |
| Incentives | What do they gain, lose, protect, or avoid? |
| Information | What is known, private, misperceived, or strategically disclosed? |
| Power and rules | Who sets rules, controls access, bears switching costs, or can wait? |
| Adaptation | What response becomes likely after each visible move? |
| Signals | Which actions are costly enough to be informative? |
| Break conditions | What observation would invalidate the mechanism? |

Select a named game only when it clarifies the structure:

- coordination or standards adoption;
- prisoner's dilemma or public-goods provision;
- signaling or screening;
- bargaining, BATNA, and ZOPA;
- principal-agent delegation;
- market entry and incumbent response;
- repeated cooperation or war of attrition.

Do not claim a Nash equilibrium unless players, strategies, payoffs, and information are specified well enough for that claim. Ordinal preferences are often more honest than invented numeric utilities.

## Human corrections

For every strategic prediction, test at least the corrections that could reverse it:

- **Bounded rationality:** attention, knowledge, and computation are limited.
- **Misperception:** actors may hold the wrong model of others or of the world.
- **Identity and values:** legitimacy, loyalty, fairness, emotion, and taboo can dominate material payoff.
- **Institutions and power:** rules, hierarchy, path dependence, and agenda control shape the game itself.
- **Routines and inertia:** organizations may repeat familiar behavior despite a nominally better move.
- **Heterogeneity:** an organization is often a coalition with conflicting internal incentives.
- **Coordination cost:** a jointly beneficial equilibrium may remain unreachable.
- **Noise and accidents:** not every action is a signal, and not every outcome is chosen.

Express the result conditionally: “If actors perceive X and institution Y remains binding, response Z becomes more likely.” Attach observable signals and break conditions. Never assign a probability merely because a strategy is a best response in the simplified model.
